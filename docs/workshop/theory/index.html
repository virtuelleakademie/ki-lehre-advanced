Theory: Cognitive Load Theory Principles

Duration: 30 minutes

Learning Objectives

By the end of this section, you will understand:

  1. The Worked Example Effect - Why studying complete solutions helps novices learn
  2. The Personalisation Effect - How familiar contexts reduce cognitive load
  3. How AI can scale these principles in education

Cognitive Load Theory Basics

Working memory is limited:

  • Can only hold 3-7 items at once
  • Gets overloaded when learning complex new material
  • This is why learning can feel overwhelming

Long-term memory is unlimited:

  • Stores knowledge as organised schemas
  • Accessed automatically when needed
  • Goal of learning: Build rich schemas in long-term memory

The challenge: How do we get information from working memory into long-term memory without overload?

Principle 1: The Worked Example Effect

The Research

“Novice learners who are given worked examples to study perform better on subsequent tests than learners who are required to solve the equivalent problems themselves.”

— NSW Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (2017, p. 7)

Why It Works

Problem-solving for novices = cognitive overload

  • Must search for solution strategies
  • Must test each strategy
  • Must monitor progress
  • Must learn the concept
  • Too much for working memory!

Studying worked examples = reduced load

  • Solution is shown
  • Can focus on understanding the pattern
  • More capacity for schema construction
  • Learning is more efficient

The Evidence

  • Effect size: 0.52 across multiple studies (Crissman, 2006)
  • Particularly strong for novices (Cooper & Sweller, 1987)
  • Diminishes as expertise grows (Kalyuga et al., 2003)

Key Insight for Today

You are novices at building educational AI tools with PydanticAI and Marimo.

Therefore: We’ll show you a complete, working solution first, then you’ll study and modify it.

Principle 2: The Personalisation Effect

The Research

“Familiar contexts require less cognitive effort to process, reducing extraneous cognitive load and improving learning outcomes.”

— Cordova & Lepper (1996)

Why It Works

Generic examples:

Calculate the correlation between Variable X and Variable Y.
X: [10, 15, 20, 25, 30]
Y: [2, 4, 6, 8, 10]
  • Must process abstract variables
  • No connection to existing knowledge
  • Higher cognitive load

Personalised examples:

You love cycling. Calculate the correlation between your
weekly training hours and your average speed:

Training hours: [10, 15, 20, 25, 30]
Average speed: [25, 27, 29, 31, 33] km/h
  • Connects to existing knowledge (cycling)
  • Easier to process (familiar context)
  • More motivation (personal relevance)
  • Lower cognitive load

The Evidence

  • Increased motivation and engagement (Cordova & Lepper, 1996)
  • Better learning outcomes when contexts are familiar
  • Particularly effective when combined with worked examples

How AI Enables This at Scale

The Traditional Challenge

Creating personalised worked examples is labor-intensive:

  • Must know each student’s interests
  • Must create unique examples for each concept
  • Must ensure quality and accuracy
  • Doesn’t scale beyond small classes

The AI Solution

Large language models can:

  • Generate unique examples on demand
  • Weave personal contexts naturally
  • Maintain pedagogical quality (with structured outputs)
  • Scale to unlimited students

Today’s Goal

Build a tool that:

  1. Collects learner interests, hobbies, goals
  2. Generates personalised worked examples
  3. Teaches concepts through familiar contexts
  4. Uses PydanticAI to ensure structured, quality output

The Workshop as Worked Example

ImportantMeta-Pedagogical Design

This workshop itself demonstrates the worked example principle:

Traditional Workshop (Problem-Solving)

"Here are the requirements. Build a personalised
example generator. We'll help when you get stuck."

→ High cognitive load, potential frustration, unclear pattern

Our Workshop (Worked Example)

"Here's a complete solution. Let's study how it works.
Now modify it for your needs."

→ Lower cognitive load, clearer pattern, better transfer

Why? Because you’re learning a new domain (educational AI tools with PydanticAI/Marimo), you’re novices. The research says worked examples are more effective than unguided problem-solving for novices.
